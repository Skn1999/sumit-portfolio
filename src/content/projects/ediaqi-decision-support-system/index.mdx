## 01 // CONTEXT

**EDIAQI** (Evidence Driven Indoor Air Quality Improvement) is a **Horizon Europe** research initiative deploying IoT-based indoor air quality (IAQ) monitoring across public schools and municipal offices in **Ferrara, Italy**. The project infrastructure includes 32 NetPID sensors and Radiello passive samplers, streaming 12+ parameters (PM2.5, PM10, CO₂, CO, TVOC, AVOC, NO₂, O₃, humidity, temperature, pressure, and formaldehyde) to a FROST Server via the OGC SensorThings API.

The Deda Next team deployed sensors to collect telemetry from participating buildings. The gap was everything that comes after the data: **what should a non-technical user actually do when a threshold is crossed?** My contribution was designing the Decision Support System (DSS) layer that bridges sensor readings and real-world human action.

---

## 02 // PROBLEM

A dashboard alone does not produce behavior change.

When CO₂ spikes in a classroom, a teacher needs to know: *open the window, for how long, and what to tell the children*. When PM2.5 is elevated across three buildings simultaneously, a facility manager needs prioritization logic, not three identical alerts. When a maintenance action is taken, the documentation trail required by Italian occupational health law (D.Lgs. 81/2008) must be low-friction enough that it actually gets executed.

## 03 // APPROACH

### Role-Separated Architecture
- **Basic View**: Designed for teachers and office workers featuring minimal parameters, plain-language action guidance, and single-location context.
- **Advanced View**: Designed for facility staff with multi-location monitoring, severity-ranked alerts, and compliance logging flows.

### Action Protocol Layer
Drawing from the Boston Public Schools IAQ Response precedent, I designed an explicit action protocol layer surfacing:
1. Plain-language severity labels
2. Context-specific action prompts (e.g., *"Open two windows — cross-ventilation recommended"*)
3. One-tap logging for compliance documentation
4. Recovery status indicators closing the feedback loop

### Heuristic Evaluation & Wall Display
Conducted a full Nielsen Heuristic Evaluation of the initial dashboard prototype, identifying 30 documented issues. Designed a 5-state wall-mounted tablet display prototype (Ambient, Alert, Action Taken, Reporting, Educational) in Figma simulating real-time telemetry changes.

## 04 // IMPACT

- **30 Usability Issues Surfaced & Resolved:** Evaluated existing dashboard against legal and accessibility standards.
- **Role-Separated DSS Framework:** Delivered lo-fi wireframes and interactive prototypes for 3 distinct stakeholder personas across public schools and municipal offices in Italy.
- **Institutional Clarity:** Identified Classe II threshold misalignments under Italian occupational health standards (D.Lgs. 81/2008), providing legal and institutional clarity for future deployments.
