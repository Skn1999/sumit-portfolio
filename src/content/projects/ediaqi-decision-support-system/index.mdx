## Why Sensor Data Wasn't Enough

As part of the **EU Horizon Europe EDIAQI project** in Ferrara, Italy, technical teams deployed 32 IoT sensors streaming 12+ real-time environmental parameters (CO₂, PM2.5, VOCs, temperature, humidity) to a central server.

Yet rich sensor data did not equate to healthier air. Non-expert occupants (teachers, municipal office workers) were expected to monitor complex telemetry while doing their daily work:

* **The Monitoring Burden**: Teachers cannot keep an admin dashboard open while managing a classroom of 25 students.
* **Alerts Without Actions**: Raw numbers (e.g. *PM2.5 = 45 µg/m³*) fail to answer basic questions: *Open which window, and for how long?*
* **Alert Fatigue**: Complex time-series graphs caused users to simply ignore alerts altogether.

> **The Guiding Belief:** Sensors detect environmental threats, but only occupants can resolve them. Decision support must live in the room where actions happen—translating raw telemetry into immediate, glanceable physical directives.

Instead of forcing occupants to log into an administrative dashboard, the core interface was moved directly into the room on a dedicated ambient tablet display:

## Testing with 200 Participants

I initially hypothesized that progressive disclosure (revealing details only on demand) would prevent visual clutter. To validate this, I ran a **2x2 Mixed Factorial Experiment** testing 6 emergency breach scenarios across 200 participants.

<div className="not-prose my-8 grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
  <div className="p-5 rounded-2xl border border-paper-border bg-paper-card">
    <div className="text-[11px] font-mono uppercase tracking-wider text-ink-muted mb-2">
      Initial Hypothesis (Disconfirmed)
    </div>
    <div className="text-sm font-semibold text-ink-primary mb-1">
      Progressive Disclosure
    </div>
    <p className="text-xs text-ink-muted leading-relaxed">
      Assumed step-by-step disclosure would reduce overwhelm. In practice, hiding action steps increased cognitive friction and delayed intervention during critical CO₂ spikes.

## Takeaways

* **Upfront beats progressive disclosure**: In time-critical situations, hiding guidance behind taps causes hesitation. Immediate answers work best.
* **Glanceability over technical precision**: Non-expert occupants need plain verbs and visual state changes, not decimal points and parts-per-million units.
* **Guidance must close the loop**: Prompting an action without signaling when to stop (e.g. closing windows after airing out a room) breaks trust and thermal comfort.
